# HorrorPlay — Narrative Overhaul

## Purpose

This document defines the narrative direction for the AA vertical slice and the full story that should grow from it. The goal is not merely to make the lore denser. The story must repeatedly **recontextualize what the player already experienced**.

The intended emotional/narrative reference points are psychological mystery, surreal small-town horror, institutional exploitation and unreliable perception. The game must remain its own story; it should not reproduce characters, plots or mythology from any reference work.

---

# 1. Audit of the current story

## What already works

The current vertical slice has several genuinely strong recurring motifs:

- Coast Guard unit **317**.
- Three bell strikes.
- A green light moving beneath the sea.
- Radio voices that know people's names.
- The Devil's Reef.
- An impossible chronology: the final Coast Guard transmission occurs after the probable time of death indicated by the case file.
- A harbor closure order dated before the 317 disappearance.
- Barnaby and Silas behaving as if the town knows more than it will admit.
- The 317 returns empty.
- A service locker and wet coat associated with the missing crew.
- Amphibious tracks moving from the sea toward land.
- A radio transmission apparently preserved after the event.

These should not be discarded. They are the foundation of the rewrite.

## Main narrative weakness

The present mystery develops almost exactly as the player predicts:

1. people disappear near a strange town;
2. locals hide information;
3. medical evidence suggests fish-like humans;
4. strange tracks appear;
5. something large is in the water;
6. there is a structure below the reef;
7. a creature or entities live there.

This produces atmosphere but not a powerful reversal. The current ending confirms the hypothesis established early in the story rather than destroying it and replacing it with a more disturbing truth.

The player currently discovers **more information**, but rarely discovers that previous information meant something completely different.

## Secondary weaknesses

### The Inspector has no dramatic wound

The protagonist currently functions as a capable camera through which the player sees the case. The mystery has professional stakes but few personal stakes.

### Innsmouth is too close to the expected hostile-cult-town archetype

Barnaby and Silas are effective scene characters, but the player currently has little reason to revise the assumption that the town is hiding its own monstrosity.

### The supernatural rules are vague

The radio, names, bells, green light, transformation and temporal inconsistency currently feel related because they are all creepy, not because the player can eventually infer a coherent rule.

### The human conspiracy is missing

The best institutional-horror stories become more disturbing when human beings discover an impossible phenomenon and make the deliberate decision to exploit it. The existing harbor closure and Coast Guard paperwork already provide an ideal entry point.

### The current Reef endings stop where the real revelation should begin

Reaching the underwater structure is currently treated as the climax. Narratively, it should be the moment when the player realizes that the original case was false.

# 2. New thematic core

## Theme

**A memory is not the same thing as a soul. But if a memory can suffer, does the distinction still matter?**

Supporting themes:

- the violence of turning human tragedy into usable information;
- whether truth is always worth preserving;
- guilt produced by knowledge;
- identity as a story that memory tells itself;
- institutions transforming the incomprehensible into a resource;
- communities that appear secretive because secrecy is the only form of containment available to them.

The story should ask the player whether investigation itself can become an act of harm.

---

# 3. The supernatural truth

## The Reef is not fundamentally a monster nest

Beneath Devil's Reef is an ancient non-human structure/organism that behaves like a **mnemonic resonator**.

It does not read minds in the conventional sense.

It records patterns carried through:

- water;
- vibration;
- human voice;
- radio transmission;
- names spoken with recognition.

A person's **name** functions as an address. When somebody hears their own name through the signal and consciously answers it, the circuit becomes significantly stronger.

This is why the warning is always:

> Do not answer if you hear your name.

The phenomenon can reproduce fragments of consciousness with terrifying precision, but it does not understand chronology the way a human observer does.

Consequently, transmissions can contain:

- memories of the dead;
- events occurring elsewhere;
- events that have not happened yet;
- voices assembled from several remembered speakers.

The signal does not deliberately lie. It simply has no concept of the difference between **memory, prediction, repetition and present experience**.

This creates the central horror:

**The dead are not haunting Innsmouth. Their memories are still being played.**

# 4. Human history: PROJECT LANTERN

## Discovery

The phenomenon was discovered decades before the 317 incident when fishermen reported hearing drowned relatives through telegraph cable and improvised radio equipment near Devil's Reef.

The town learned a primitive containment rule:

- do not answer;
- do not speak the names of the drowned near the water;
- do not amplify the signal;
- close the Reef during certain tides;
- destroy or remove records that allow outsiders to reconstruct the route.

This transforms apparently irrational local superstition into survival knowledge.

## Institutional exploitation

During and after the Great War, a federal/coastal intelligence program discovers the reports.

Internal designation:

**PROJECT LANTERN**

Publicly it is coastal acoustic research.

Privately, the objective becomes horrifyingly practical:

> Can the Reef retrieve information from dead men?

Investigators discover that the signal can reproduce fragments of memories belonging to sailors and agents who died carrying information.

The program attempts to use the Reef as an intelligence archive of the dead.

This is the human sin at the center of the story.

## The pathology research

The 1898 pathology monograph was never merely an eccentric medical paper.

Its subjects were families with unusually high sensitivity to the Reef signal.

The so-called Innsmouth traits are partly hereditary adaptations and partly the cumulative biological effects of repeated exposure.

Later PROJECT LANTERN researchers quietly acquire the author's notes and use the families as a population of natural receivers.

This changes the moral reading of Innsmouth:

The people are not simply hiding that they are monsters.

Many are descendants of people **studied, used and exposed** because outsiders discovered that their bodies could hear the Reef better.

---

# 5. What really happened to Unit 317

The 317 patrol was **not an accident**.

The official order said they were investigating unauthorized lights.

The hidden mission was a PROJECT LANTERN field test.

The three guards are:

- **Hale** — ordinary Coast Guard crewman; skeptic; genuine victim.
- **Mercer** — Innsmouth-born or descended from an Innsmouth family; selected because records indicate unusually high signal sensitivity. He is never told why he was selected.
- **Ward** — technically part of the crew but secretly briefed by the program. He carries sealed instructions and is told to activate/recover equipment at the Reef.

Ward believes this is a controlled intelligence experiment.

It is not.

The program intentionally sends a natural receiver (Mercer) into the strongest recorded signal window.

The experiment succeeds beyond expectation.

That is why the harbor had already been closed before the patrol disappeared: the closure was part of the test perimeter.

The municipal seal was removed later to hide who authorized it.

---

# 6. The first major reversal: Innsmouth was trying to stop the investigation

For the first act, the player should reasonably believe:

> The town is protecting whatever lives at the Reef.

Midway through the story this interpretation breaks.

Barnaby, Silas and several residents are not protecting the Reef.

They are protecting everyone else **from people who keep reopening it**.

Barnaby's hostility toward investigators is not cult loyalty. He remembers previous government teams arriving with paperwork, equipment and promises that nothing will happen.

Silas has seen the same pattern.

The town removes seals, loses keys, lies about routes and refuses to say names because bureaucratic ignorance is safer than official curiosity.

This makes earlier scenes emotionally different on replay.

Barnaby was not saying:

> Stay away from our secret.

He was saying:

> Please stop doing this to us.

---

# 7. The second major reversal: the 317 recording is not a recording

This reveal should occur after the current Boathouse section or at the beginning of the Reef facility chapter.

The player initially believes the radio contains the preserved final transmission of unit 317.

Later, technical evidence proves something impossible:

**there is no recording medium connected to the radio.**

The transmission is live.

Not live in geographic space.

Live through the Reef.

The signal contains phrases spoken by the Inspector **during the present investigation**, after the 317 incident supposedly occurred.

Examples should be subtle before the reveal:

- a word Barnaby used earlier that day;
- the Inspector's cough;
- the exact sound of the office drawer opening;
- a line the player chose in dialogue, almost inaudible under static.

This is the moment where the player understands:

> The radio is not replaying the past. The Reef is collapsing memories from different times into the same signal.

The existing autopsy contradiction becomes crucial evidence rather than flavor text.

One guard really was dead before part of the transmission attributed to him was broadcast.

---

# 8. The Inspector's personal connection

The Inspector should not secretly be an ancient fish god, a clone, or a missing guard. That would make the mystery smaller.

His connection should be human and emotionally legible.

## Recommended backstory

Years earlier, before becoming an investigator, the Inspector lost someone close to water — preferably a younger brother or childhood friend.

He remembers the event as a simple drowning.

There is one detail he has never told anyone:

After the person disappeared beneath the surface, the Inspector heard them call his name from somewhere impossible.

He answered.

Nothing apparently happened.

He has spent his adult life interpreting that memory as shock and guilt.

PROJECT LANTERN's surviving administrators know about the incident because the drowning occurred in a region where early signal anomalies were documented.

The Inspector was not randomly assigned case 47-B.

He was selected as another potential receiver.

The investigation itself is a test.

This betrayal gives the conspiracy personal weight without making the Inspector secretly superhuman.

---

# 9. The third major reversal: the voice in the cold open is the player

This is the central recontextualizing twist and must be prepared carefully.

In the cold open, the 317 hears an unknown signal speaking:

> Hale... Mercer... Ward...

The crew assumes that something beneath the water knows their names.

At the climax, after entering the Reef signal chamber, the Inspector hears the cold-open radio exchange occurring around him as if it were happening now.

He realizes he can transmit through the structure.

He knows the crew's names because he has spent the entire game investigating them.

He desperately tries to warn them.

He keys the transmitter and says:

> Hale. Mercer. Ward. Listen to me. Do not answer the voices. Turn the 317 around.

But time and signal degradation cut the message apart.

Four nights earlier the crew only heard:

> ...Hale... Mercer... Ward...

The ominous unknown voice in the opening was **the Inspector trying to save them**.

His warning is one of the events that frightened the crew into reacting exactly as documented.

The player has not merely discovered the case.

By pursuing the case to its source, the player became part of its cause.

This should be emotionally devastating, not presented as a clever time-loop gimmick.

The question becomes:

> If trying to prevent a tragedy is one of the things that causes it, what does responsibility mean?

---

# 10. Important limitation: not a conventional reset loop

The story should NOT become a game about endlessly repeating the same day.

Time itself is not globally looping.

The Reef is creating a **causal acoustic bridge** between moments stored in its mnemonic field.

Outside the signal, ordinary time continues normally.

This keeps the supernatural rule distinctive and prevents the plot from collapsing into generic time-loop fiction.

---

# 11. The deeper truth of the 'creatures'

Do not fully explain whether every amphibious figure is the same phenomenon.

Evidence should support at least two interpretations:

### Interpretation A — biological descendants

Generations of exposure changed some Innsmouth bloodlines. They are living humans with unusual adaptations.

### Interpretation B — mnemonic reconstruction

Some things emerging from the water are incomplete physical reconstructions created from recorded human patterns.

They resemble people because the Reef is trying to reproduce people from memory without understanding anatomy perfectly.

The most disturbing encounters should blur these categories.

A figure may wear a dead fisherman's jacket and remember his daughter's name while possessing anatomy that cannot be human.

The story should never reduce this to a bestiary taxonomy.

---

# 12. Character overhaul

## Barnaby

Surface role:

Hostile tavern keeper withholding access.

True role:

A practical containmentist. His family has spent generations keeping outsiders away from the Reef.

His key is not merely a quest item. Giving it to the Inspector is an ethical surrender.

If persuaded gently, later scenes reveal that Barnaby genuinely hoped this investigator might be different.

If threatened, his apparent menace becomes tragic: the Inspector has repeated the exact behavior of earlier government agents.

## Silas

Surface role:

Cryptic old fisherman.

True role:

A survivor/witness of an earlier PROJECT LANTERN incident, possibly as a young deckhand or shore radio assistant.

He does not speak in riddles because he is a mystical exposition machine. He speaks carefully because he learned that names and detailed recollection can strengthen the signal.

His evasiveness has a physical reason.

A powerful late reveal would be that Silas never asks the Inspector's name — intentionally.

## Mercer

Mercer becomes the emotional center of the 317 tragedy.

He was selected because of his family, not his competence.

PROJECT LANTERN effectively used him as human equipment.

His final messages should gradually shift from fear to the realization that Ward knew more than he admitted.

## Ward

Ward should not be a cartoon villain.

He believed the experiment was safe and potentially valuable.

His guilt in the final transmission is important.

He eventually tries to shut the system down and removes the Boathouse fuse as part of that attempt.

This recontextualizes the missing fuse:

It was not random sabotage.

Ward removed it to prevent the equipment from being reactivated.

The player later reverses Ward's last protective act when installing the fuse.

That is excellent dramatic irony and should be explicitly revealed later.

## Hale

Hale is the ordinary person inside the nightmare.

Letters or photographs should make him feel like a person before the player learns his fate.

Without this, the missing guards remain plot tokens.

# 13. Recontextualizing existing evidence

Every major clue should have an initial interpretation and a later interpretation.

| Evidence | Initial interpretation | Later truth |
|---|---|---|
| Autopsy timing | sloppy local coroner / cover-up | transmission contains a dead man's later memory because chronology is collapsing |
| Harbor closure | locals knew about monster | PROJECT LANTERN established a controlled test perimeter |
| Barnaby's key | town is hiding access | Barnaby is trying to prevent another activation |
| 317 manifest | routine patrol record | crew composition was deliberately engineered |
| Mercer | unlucky guard | selected as biological receiver |
| Ward | missing victim | covert program participant who later tried to abort mission |
| Removed fuse | sabotage by creature/cult | Ward disabled the system to prevent reactivation |
| Radio log | preserved recording | live mnemonic transmission crossing time |
| Voices calling names | predatory supernatural entity | sometimes the Reef; sometimes reconstructed dead; in the cold open, the Inspector himself |
| Amphibious figures | monsters from Reef | mixture of altered descendants and/or imperfect human reconstructions |
| Innsmouth secrecy | cult conspiracy | intergenerational containment response to outside exploitation |

This table is the narrative standard for all new clues.

A clue that has only one meaning is usually exposition. A strong mystery clue should become more interesting after the player learns the truth.

# 14. Revised act structure

## ACT I — THE OFFICIAL STORY

Locations mostly already exist:

- cold open 317;
- Inspector office;
- Marsh Street;
- Tavern;
- Docks.

Player belief:

> Three Coast Guard officers found something in a town hiding an amphibious cult.

Important new seeds:

- case-file handwriting/order inconsistencies;
- Mercer specifically requested for patrol despite another crewman being available;
- Barnaby refuses to say names near an open window;
- Silas never asks the Inspector's name;
- harbor order classification code that does not belong to municipal government;
- Ward's signature appears on a hidden equipment receipt.

## ACT II — THE FALSE MONSTER

Current Boathouse material expanded.

Player belief:

> The government and town both knew creatures existed at the Reef.

Revelations:

- Ward had classified instructions;
- the fuse was deliberately removed from inside;
- radio has no recording mechanism;
- the signal contains an impossible present-day sound;
- Mercer was deliberately selected;
- first direct evidence of PROJECT LANTERN.

End of act revelation:

> The patrol was an experiment.

## ACT III — PROJECT LANTERN

New location after the boat journey:

- abandoned coastal listening station / stone structure integrated into Reef;
- 1920s equipment bolted into architecture far older than human settlement;
- medical records;
- acoustic experiment logs;
- names carved into walls by people trying not to say them aloud.

The player learns the town was containment, not conspiracy.

The institution that assigned the case becomes the human antagonist.

End of act revelation:

> The 317 transmission is still happening.

## ACT IV — THE ARCHIVE

The Reef interior becomes progressively impossible, but spatial surrealism must follow memory logic rather than random dream imagery.

Rooms contain fragments of:

- the 317;
- the Inspector's office;
- Barnaby's tavern;
- the Inspector's childhood drowning memory;
- PROJECT LANTERN experiments.

They overlap because the Reef indexes events through people and names rather than places.

The player realizes the signal knows the Inspector because he answered it years ago.

End of act revelation:

> The Inspector was intentionally assigned because he was already signal-sensitive.

## ACT V — THE COLD OPEN RETURNS

The player reaches the core/transmitter.

The original 317 exchange plays again, now live around the player.

The player attempts to warn Hale, Mercer and Ward.

The transmission degrades into the exact unknown voice from the beginning.

Final revelation:

> The player has been listening to himself since the first scene.

This is where the title/central motif should acquire its second meaning.

# 15. Final decisions

The endings must not be simple good / neutral / evil branches.

Each should resolve one value while violating another.

## ENDING A — SILENCE

Requirement style:

High investigation completeness + player rejects institutional logic.

The Inspector destroys the human amplification equipment and burns/destroys the case archive.

PROJECT LANTERN loses the route and the Reef becomes difficult to access again.

Cost:

The official truth disappears too.

Hale, Mercer and Ward remain a bureaucratic accident.

Families receive no real explanation.

As records burn, the Inspector begins forgetting the exact sound of the drowned person from his childhood.

Final question:

> Is forgetting a mercy if memory itself is the doorway?

## ENDING B — WITNESS

The Inspector broadcasts the PROJECT LANTERN files and proof of the experiment to authorities/newspapers before destroying the site.

The conspiracy cannot easily bury the truth.

For a moment this feels like the morally clean ending.

Then radios across Massachusetts begin receiving fragments of the broadcast.

In separate homes, listeners hear familiar dead voices whisper their names.

The evidence carried the signal out with it.

Truth survives.

Containment does not.

## ENDING C — ANSWER

The Inspector tries to use the mnemonic bridge to save the 317.

He becomes the unknown voice in the cold open.

His intervention cannot erase the event because it was already part of it.

Instead, the Reef absorbs more of his memory until his voice can be heard across multiple moments.

The ending returns to the opening shot.

This time the player understands every line.

The title card appears only after the loop closes.

## SECRET / HIGH-EVIDENCE ENDING — THE FOURTH NAME

Optional, if the full game needs a deeper final reward.

The player notices that PROJECT LANTERN records consistently refer to **four signal signatures** aboard the 317 although only three men were present.

The fourth is not a hidden crewman.

It is the Inspector's future transmission, detected by instruments during the original experiment.

PROJECT LANTERN knew before assigning case 47-B that the Inspector would eventually reach the Reef.

They did not merely choose him because he was sensitive.

They chose him because his future voice was already in their archived 317 data.

The institution assigned him the case because the evidence proved he was going to take it.

This makes the case itself a bootstrap paradox and makes the human conspiracy even more disturbing:

They exploit not only people, but causal inevitability.

The secret ending should allow the Inspector to act on this knowledge in a way PROJECT LANTERN did not predict — not by magically rewriting time, but by denying them usable information.

# 16. Foreshadowing rules

Every major twist must be fair.

The player should be able to replay the game and say:

> It was there the whole time.

Required foreshadowing:

### For the temporal signal

- autopsy/transmission time contradiction;
- clocks/radio timestamps disagree by recurring intervals;
- present-day sounds buried inside old radio signal;
- wet objects that should have dried;
- phrases repeated before their apparent first use.

### For PROJECT LANTERN

- non-municipal classification codes;
- federal paper stock/stamps;
- equipment too advanced/expensive for a small Coast Guard shed;
- Ward's unexplained technical inventory;
- Mercer specifically requested by name;
- pathology paper referenced by an internal project code.

### For the town-as-containment reversal

- residents avoid saying names rather than merely avoiding questions;
- Barnaby closes windows before discussing the Reef;
- Silas warns against bringing evidence back to town;
- locals sabotage equipment rather than worship it;
- hostile actions consistently make access harder, never lead the player toward a ritual.

### For the Inspector-as-cold-open-voice twist

- cold-open voice should have cadence/noise matching the Inspector's radio later;
- one syllable/word the Inspector often uses can survive distortion;
- the cold open should NOT identify the signal as a monster in narration;
- at least one crewman says the voice sounds 'almost human' or 'like someone on another frequency'.

Do not make this clue obvious on first play.

---

# 17. Surrealism guidelines

The game can become stranger without becoming arbitrary.

Surreal events must be **memory collisions**, not random spooky imagery.

Good examples:

- the Office drawer appears inside the Reef chamber because its opening sound exists in the signal;
- a Tavern conversation plays from a Coast Guard radio before the conversation occurs;
- a corridor's wallpaper briefly matches the Inspector's childhood home because his own memory has entered the field;
- one missing guard repeats a line the Inspector has not said yet;
- a room changes only after the player speaks/examines a name.

Bad examples:

- random floating furniture;
- unexplained blood hallways just because they are scary;
- dream sequences with no later causal meaning;
- monsters that appear only for jumpscares and never connect to the rules.

---

# 18. Sanity system narrative role

Sanity should not mean 'the lower the meter, the less true the world becomes.'

That would let the player dismiss all unusual information as hallucination.

Instead:

- high sanity = the Inspector filters the signal and notices objective inconsistencies;
- low sanity = the Inspector becomes a stronger receiver and can perceive additional mnemonic material;
- both states reveal **different true information**;
- neither state gives the complete truth alone.

This makes low sanity narratively useful rather than simply failure.

Example:

High sanity player notices a timestamp impossibility.

Low sanity player hears the future sentence hidden inside the same recording.

Only by comparing evidence can the player understand what happened.

---

# 19. Investigation system upgrade

The casebook should eventually support **recontextualized evidence**.

An evidence entry can change after a revelation.

Example:

Initial:

> LAST TRANSMISSION — Final recorded message from unit 317.

After discovering no recorder exists:

> LAST TRANSMISSION — Signal attributed to unit 317. There is no physical recording source. The word 'recording' in the original report is unsupported.

After temporal reveal:

> 317 SIGNAL — Contains events from at least three different chronological points.

The player should visually feel the case theory being dismantled and reconstructed.

---

# 20. Narrative design rule going forward

Before adding any major clue, character or horror beat, answer four questions:

1. What does the player think this means when they first see it?
2. What does it actually mean?
3. Which later revelation changes its meaning?
4. What human consequence does that change create?

If questions 2–4 have weak answers, the clue probably does not deserve major screen time.

---

# 21. Immediate rewrite priorities for the current vertical slice

Do NOT rebuild every room before the narrative foundation is locked.

The highest-value changes are:

1. Rewrite cold-open phrasing so the unknown signal remains interpretable as a human transmission.
2. Strengthen the autopsy time contradiction in the Office.
3. Add one PROJECT LANTERN breadcrumb to the Office archive.
4. Make Silas conspicuously avoid names.
5. Make Barnaby's behavior read as containment in hindsight.
6. Recontextualize the removed Boathouse fuse as deliberate protective sabotage by Ward.
7. Change the radio so the player hears one impossible present-day sound/phrase inside the alleged old transmission.
8. Replace the current Reef 'monster confirmation' ending with the first true narrative reversal: discovery of human listening equipment integrated into the ancient structure.
9. End the vertical slice on a transmission containing a line the Inspector has not spoken yet.
10. Do NOT reveal the final cold-open voice twist inside the vertical slice. Save it for the full-game climax.

---

# 22. Proposed vertical-slice ending

The current slice should no longer conclude with only:

> There is a structure inside the Reef.

Recommended new final beat:

The Inspector reaches the structure and finds a corroded listening room built partly by humans, partly around impossible black stone.

A reel-to-reel/recording apparatus is present — but disconnected and empty.

A headset begins playing the 317 transmission anyway.

The player hears the familiar warning.

Then, under the static, a new sentence appears in the Inspector's own voice:

> "No abras el archivo todavía."

It is a sentence the player has never heard or spoken.

The Inspector freezes.

A typewritten PROJECT LANTERN sheet beside the equipment lists four detected signal signatures:

- HALE
- MERCER
- WARD
- UNKNOWN / MATCH PENDING

The final line reads:

> MATCH SOURCE: CASE 47-B INVESTIGATOR — PREDICTED ARRIVAL WINDOW: [current date/time of story]

Cut to black.

This delivers a genuine vertical-slice hook without spending the final reveal that the unknown voice is the Inspector himself.

---

# 23. Final narrative target

The finished story should cause three major belief changes:

### At the beginning

> Innsmouth is hiding monsters.

### Mid-game

> Innsmouth is hiding a government experiment built around something it does not understand.

### Late-game

> The government experiment is exploiting a phenomenon that records people outside chronological order — and the investigation itself is already inside that record.

### Climax

> The voice that began the nightmare was the player trying to stop it.

The best ending is not the one where the Inspector kills the largest creature.

It is the one where the player finally understands that **the case has been listening to them investigate it**.
